2. DATA AND THE INITIAL MODEL

Use only the first five columns of aadt.txt. There are 121 complete observations.
AADT ranges from 201 to 155547 vehicles/day. All responses and populations are
positive, so logarithms and positive root transformations are defined.

The original control code is 1 for access control and 2 for no access control.
Define access=1 for original code 1, and access=0 for original code 2.
There are 27 controlled-access and 94 uncontrolled-access road sections.

The scatterplot matrix shows positive associations of traffic with population,
lanes and access control. Width has a much weaker marginal relationship.
Population and AADT are strongly skewed. Lanes and access control are associated:
all two-lane sections lack access control, and sections above four lanes all have
access control. Do not interpret their fitted coefficients as causal effects.

Start with the required four predictors:
  AADT = beta0 + beta1*population + beta2*lanes + beta3*width + beta4*access + error.

M1 has R-squared 0.7527 and adjusted R-squared 0.7442. Its overall F-test is highly
significant. Nevertheless, the residual plot shows curvature and increasing spread,
the Q-Q plot has substantial tail departures, and 45 fitted AADT values are negative.
This is a useful baseline, but it is not an adequate final model.

3. COMPLETE RECORD OF WHAT WORKED AND WHAT DID NOT

Every model below is printed in R_output.txt, saved in all_candidate_models.rds,
and has its own residual figure in coursework_results/plots/.
Model numbering preserves the exploratory record; it is not a ranking.

M1_raw
  Response: AADT. Predictors: population, lanes, width, access.
  Purpose: required initial model.
  Result: explains some variation but has curvature, strongly unequal residual
  spread, tail problems and negative fitted values. Retained as the baseline.

M2_log_y
  Response: log(AADT). Predictors: population, lanes, width, access.
  Purpose: compress high responses to reduce increasing variance.
  Result: Q-Q plot improves substantially, but the residual-versus-fitted trend
  curves and turns downward at large fitted values. Transforming y alone is insufficient.

M3_log_y_log_population
  Response: log(AADT). Predictors: log(population), lanes, width, access.
  Purpose: allow a nonlinear relationship with population.
  Result: improves the population specification, but residual curvature and
  changing spread remain, with influential tail observations.

M4_log_y_log_population_lanes
  Response: log(AADT). Predictors: log(population), log(lanes), width, access.
  Purpose: allow a diminishing relationship with lane count on the log-response scale.
  Result: the mean trend is flatter, but residual spread decreases at larger fitted
  values. It improves on earlier log models but does not fully resolve the variance issue.

M5_log_y_quadratic_lanes
  Response: log(AADT). Predictors: log(population), lanes, lanes^2, width, access.
  Purpose: a polynomial alternative, explicitly covered in Chapter 4 Part II.
  Result: adding lanes^2 to M3 helps (nested F-test p=0.00305), but decreasing
  residual spread remains. Keep both lanes and lanes^2; a significant extra term
  does not by itself make the entire model adequate.

M6_log_y_reduced
  Response: log(AADT). Predictors: log(population), log(lanes), access.
  Purpose: test whether width adds useful information to M4.
  Result: width deletion is defensible (F-test p=0.531), but the residual spread
  problem persists. Simplification does not repair an unsuitable response transformation.

M7_fourth_root_full
  Response: AADT^0.25. Predictors: log(population), log(lanes), width, access.
  Purpose: a positive power between log and square root; equivalently sqrt(sqrt(AADT)).
  Result: substantially more stable residual spread and a much straighter Q-Q plot.
  This is a reasonable candidate, not a failed model.

M8_square_root_full
  Response: sqrt(AADT). Predictors: log(population), log(lanes), width, access.
  Purpose: try the standard square-root response transformation shown in the slides.
  Result: compresses large observations, but noticeable curvature and an increasing
  residual spread remain. It does not compress the high traffic values sufficiently.

M9_fourth_root_reduced
  Response: AADT^0.25. Predictors: log(population), log(lanes), access.
  Purpose: simplify M7 after checking width.
  Result: width is not supported (F-test p=0.397); residual checks remain reasonable.
  This remains a valid alternative. Its back-transformed prediction is about 5728.

M10_fifth_root_full
  Response: AADT^0.2. Predictors: log(population), log(lanes), width, access.
  Purpose: try a nearby power that compresses the response slightly more than a fourth root.
  Result: a relatively flat residual trend, approximately stable spread, and a
  reasonably straight Q-Q plot, with a few tail observations still needing discussion.

M11_fifth_root_reduced -- SELECTED WORKING MODEL
  Response: AADT^0.2. Predictors: log(population), log(lanes), access.
  Purpose: simplify M10 without losing a supported predictor.
  Result: the width F-test gives F(1,116)=0.6317, p=0.4284. At the 5% level there
  is insufficient evidence that width improves the model given the other predictors.
  Residuals versus the omitted width do not show an obvious remaining trend.
  All retained predictor coefficients have small t-test p-values.

4. WHY THIS MODEL WAS CHOSEN, AND WHAT THE CHOICE DOES NOT MEAN

The main reasoning follows the lectures:
1) The raw model has clear diagnostic defects; significance and R-squared alone
   do not rescue it.
2) Log response models improve the tails but leave curvature or uneven spread.
   The square-root model leaves increasing spread. A power between these two
   transformation strengths is a reasonable next trial.
3) Both fourth-root and fifth-root models give substantially more satisfactory
   residual checks. M11 is retained as a defensible working choice; its residual
   smooth is close to horizontal. It is NOT proved uniquely best by these plots.
4) The full/reduced F-test supports dropping width. An additional joint test of
   squared log-population and squared log-lane terms gives p=0.481, so the simple
   mean specification is retained. These are nested comparisons with the SAME response.
5) Keep all observations, discuss the unusual cases, and report the sensitivity.

Honest provenance: the earlier extended exploration used Box-Cox and cross-validation
to investigate nearby powers. The Box-Cox maximum was near 0.18; a fifth root is a
convenient nearby power. Those techniques are preserved in the archive but are not
needed to run or explain the main lecture-level workflow. The slides do not prescribe
the exact fifth-root transformation: it is an extension of their transformation idea,
also permitted by the assignment. Do not claim it was specified in the syllabus or
chosen before seeing the data. The fourth-root alternative is also defensible.

Do not compare raw-model R-squared with fifth-root-model R-squared as if the response
were unchanged. Do not run an F-test between models on different response scales.
Do not say width has no real-world effect: the result concerns its additional linear
contribution in this fitted model. Exploratory selection makes subsequent textbook
intervals conditional on the chosen specification, not a guarantee about all possible models.

5. FINAL FIT AND INTERPRETATION

Let Z = AADT^(1/5). The fitted conditional mean of Z is
  Z_hat = -2.739015 + 0.535189*log(population)
                     + 2.317245*log(lanes) + 1.106497*access.
All logarithms are natural logarithms.

R-squared = 0.8528; adjusted R-squared = 0.8490, both on the fifth-root scale.
Residual standard error = 0.7478, with 117 residual degrees of freedom.
Overall F(3,117) is approximately 226.0, with p<2.2e-16.

Holding the other fitted predictors fixed, doubling population changes fitted Z by
0.535189*log(2), about 0.371. Doubling lane count changes fitted Z by
2.317245*log(2), about 1.606. Access control is associated with a 1.1065 higher
fitted Z at the same population and lane count. These are transformed-scale changes,
not fixed increases in vehicles/day or direct percentage changes in AADT.
The intercept is not useful as a physical prediction outside the observed settings.

Residuals are substantially improved, but not perfect. Row 93 has the largest Cook's
distance: AADT=756, population=941411, lanes=2, width=24, no access control.
Row 92 also has a large positive residual. There is no evidence here that either is
a data-entry error. Keep them. Omitting row 93 only for sensitivity changes the
requested back-transformed point from 5494 to 5430, about 1.2%, and the retained
coefficient signs stay positive. This checks one influential case, not every possible
joint-deletion scenario.

The file-order Durbin-Watson statistic is 1.7726. Its computation follows the tutorial,
but the dataset description does not establish time or spatial ordering. Therefore
do not interpret this value as proving independent errors. Possible clustering of
roads in a county cannot be resolved from the supplied first-five-column description.

6. REQUESTED PREDICTION

Population=50000; lanes=3; width=60; original access code=2, hence access=0.
Width does not enter M11 after the simplification test.

Fitted mean FIFTH-ROOT response: 5.59736.
95% confidence interval for mean FIFTH-ROOT response: [5.40290, 5.79183].
95% individual prediction interval on the FIFTH-ROOT scale: [4.10375, 7.09098].

Raise the fitted value and individual prediction endpoints to power 5:
  Back-transformed typical AADT: approximately 5494 vehicles/day.
  Approximate 95% individual-road prediction interval: [1164, 17928] vehicles/day.

The back-transformed point is an approximate conditional median under the normal
transformed-error model. It is NOT the arithmetic conditional mean AADT.
Likewise, raising the mean-root confidence limits to power 5 would NOT produce a
confidence interval for arithmetic mean AADT. The course-level output therefore
labels the mean-response confidence interval on its fitted fifth-root scale and
reports the individual prediction interval in original units.

The earlier extended output estimated mean AADT at about 6449 using residual
smearing and a bootstrap interval. That is a different target and method, not a
contradiction of the 5494 typical-value prediction. It is archived, not included
as a required technique in the simplified workflow.

Three lanes is not observed in the data; this prediction interpolates between the
two- and four-lane observations. The wide prediction interval reflects substantial
road-to-road variability. Its nominal coverage depends on approximate normality,
constant error variance and independence on the fitted scale.
